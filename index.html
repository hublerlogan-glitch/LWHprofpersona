Professional Evolution Analysis

During our multi week team case study on La Colombe, our team conducted three rounds of anonymous peer evaluations. Round 1 and 2 measured our initial working dynamics midway through the project, and Round 3 evaluated our final project deliverables and growth. Below is how my self perception aligned with my team feedback.

Domain 1: Core Values | Structure and Standards Drive My Work

What I Stand For

I value clear structure, personal accountability, and high standards. Stability scored 80 out of 100 on my self inventory, followed by achievement at 60.

What My Team Saw

My teammates completely agreed. All seven words I chose for myself were independently selected by my peers, including Driven, Rule Follower, and Good with Numbers. In our final project evaluation (Round 3), my work quality rating reached 95.8 out of 100.

Why It Matters for Accounting

Public accounting depends on consistency, compliance, and accuracy. Knowing that my personal standards translate into dependable team results gives my future firm a reliable foundation they can count on.

Domain 2: Communication Style | Focused Mindset, Perceived as Quiet

The Gap Between Intention and Impact

I view myself as direct and results focused, but my teammates initially saw me as quiet and analytical. Teammates noted that I can be reserved early on before becoming fully engaged in the work.

Feedback from Teammates

My goal was to keep team interactions smooth and positive, but the result was that my team wanted more direct and specific feedback from me during our project work.

My Plan: Warm and Specific Communication

To give my team the clear feedback they need while keeping a supportive environment, I am following three habits: bring one specific recommendation to every meeting, state project needs early with clear deadlines, and follow up to ensure full alignment.

Domain 3: Course Feedback | Combining Warmth with Direct Accountability

Team Support and Contribution

In our final project evaluation (Round 3), my effort score reached 99.6 out of 100 and my team environment rating reached 99.4 out of 100. All five team members agreed that I contributed extra share to our shared deliverables.

Holding Others Accountable

My rating for holding others accountable shifted from 3.8 to 3.2 out of 5 across the evaluation rounds. This highlighted my main growth goal: pairing my supportive work style with clear, direct communication when timelines or goals shift.

Real World Application

During our third team presentation, I was out of town during crucial preparation time. I stayed fully available on video calls, matched my in person effort, and asked clear questions to keep us aligned. In public accounting, combining approachable warmth with straightforward accountability builds trust with both clients and teammates.

Domain 4: Experiential Reflection | Course Work, Challenges, and Core Drivers

Prioritization Under Pressure

Managing a much larger course load in Eller taught me a ton about the skill of prioritization. Navigating high expectations showed me that I can handle far more complex responsibilities than I originally thought possible.

Communication Over Solitary Workload

The hardest part when facing project deadlines is initiating difficult conversations. Through our team projects, I learned that results are significantly better when I actively communicate and delegate rather than trying to take on all the workload by myself.

My Core Drivers: Earning a CPA to Help People

I want to earn my CPA because it gives me the credibility and technical ability to help people navigate financial struggles. Whether assisting individual taxpayers, non profit organizations, startups, or large public companies, my goal is to use my accounting expertise to support others. Outside of my career focus, playing guitar, playing chess, and serving in fraternity leadership keep me grounded and energized.
